Where Human Judgment Matters

Every submodule so far has shown a generative mechanism producing options quickly. None of them decide which option is good. That gap is exactly where human judgment lives, and it doesn't shrink as the models get better at generating — it just moves the bottleneck from "produce a draft" to "evaluate the drafts."

  • Taste — choosing which of many generated options is actually good.
  • Original concept — the model can't decide what story is worth telling or what problem a design should solve.
  • Cultural and contextual nuance — models trained mostly on global/Western data often miss local context, references, and language nuance that a human creator supplies.

Visual model: make the call yourself

These four taglines all came from the same well-specified prompt in the Writing & Prompt Iteration submodule. There's no hidden "correct" answer here — pick the one you'd actually ship.

If you compared notes with someone else who did this exercise, you might well have picked differently — and neither of you would be wrong. That's the point: taste doesn't converge to one right answer the way a math problem does, which is exactly why it isn't something a model can compute for you.
