Visual Art & Diffusion Models

Diffusion models (like those behind popular image generators) turn a text description into an image by starting from random noise and gradually refining it to match the prompt. Designers use these for rapid concept exploration — mood boards, style variations, and rough visual drafts — before committing time to a final polished version by hand or in traditional tools.

The core idea: denoise, step by step

A diffusion model is trained to do one narrow thing extremely well: given a noisy image and a text prompt, predict a slightly less noisy version of it. Run that step repeatedly — each time feeding the output back in as the new input — and a coherent image gradually emerges from what started as pure static. No single step "draws" the picture; the picture is the accumulated result of many small denoising steps.

This is a genuinely different mechanism from the classification/detection covered in Computer Vision Fundamentals — those models take an image in and produce a label out; a diffusion model takes noise (and a prompt) in and produces an image out.
