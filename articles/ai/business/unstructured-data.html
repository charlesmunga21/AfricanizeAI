Understanding Unstructured Data

Most business data isn't in neat rows and columns — it's emails, contracts, call transcripts, product photos, satellite imagery. AI models (NLP for text, computer vision for images) extract structured information from this: summarizing contracts, classifying support emails by intent, detecting product defects from photos on a production line.

The core mechanism: classification

A huge share of "AI reads your documents" products reduce to the same underlying task as the image classification covered in the Computer Vision module — just applied to text instead of pixels: take an unstructured input, output one of a fixed set of labels. A real production system learns this mapping from thousands of labeled examples; the visual model below uses simple keyword rules instead, to make the underlying idea — text in, category out — visible without needing a trained model.

Visual model: classify a ticket

Type your own support-ticket text, or try an example, and see it get routed to a category based on the words it contains. A real system would use a trained model instead of a keyword list, but the shape of the task — and why some inputs are ambiguous — is the same.

Notice what happens when nothing matches — a well-designed system doesn't guess with false confidence, it routes the ambiguous case to a human. The keyword rule here is a simplification of a much more capable trained classifier, but the same "route what you're unsure about" principle applies to the real thing.
