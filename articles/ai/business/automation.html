Automating Repetitive Decisions

Tasks that follow a pattern a human could describe but do too slowly at volume: fraud transaction flagging, loan pre-screening, invoice/document processing, customer support ticket routing. Automation here works best combined with a human-in-the-loop for edge cases and low-confidence predictions.

The threshold is the real decision

A fraud-detection model doesn't output "fraud" or "not fraud" — it outputs a risk score. Someone still has to pick the cutoff: flag everything above score X for review, auto-approve everything below. Set the threshold too low and you flood a human reviewer with false alarms; set it too high and real fraud slips through unflagged. There's no threshold that eliminates both kinds of mistakes — moving it only trades one kind for the other.

This tradeoff has names: a lower threshold catches more true fraud (higher recall) at the cost of more false alarms (lower precision); a higher threshold does the reverse. Which side to favor is a business decision, not a modeling one — it depends on how expensive a false alarm is versus a missed case.

Visual model: move the threshold yourself

Each box is one transaction, labeled with its risk score. Drag the threshold and watch which transactions get auto-flagged (amber = the auto-decision was wrong for that transaction) versus auto-approved.
