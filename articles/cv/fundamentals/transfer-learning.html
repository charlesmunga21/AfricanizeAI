Transfer Learning & Applications

You rarely train a CNN from scratch today. Pretrained backbones (ResNet, EfficientNet, ViT) already know general visual features — edges, textures, shapes — from millions of images. You fine-tune them on your specific dataset instead, which needs far less data and compute than starting from random weights.

Why fine-tuning works

Early convolution layers in a trained network tend to learn very generic features (edges, corners, color gradients) that are useful for almost any visual task, not just the one the network was originally trained on. Fine-tuning keeps those early layers mostly intact and retrains only the later, more task-specific layers on your data — so the network isn't learning "what an edge is" from zero, it's already starting from a network that understands images in general.

Where it shows up in practice

Computer vision powers crop health monitoring from drone or satellite imagery, quality inspection on manufacturing lines, medical image analysis (X-rays, retinal scans), license plate recognition, and mobile document scanning apps — among many other applications covered in the AI for Business module.
