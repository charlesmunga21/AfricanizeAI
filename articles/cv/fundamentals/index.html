Computer Vision Fundamentals

Computer vision is the field of teaching computers to extract meaning from images and video — going from raw pixel grids to information like "there is a car here" or "this is a picture of a maize field under drought stress." A model never "sees" a picture the way a person does; it operates purely on arrays of numbers.

This module goes from that raw representation up to how a real vision system gets built in practice: what an image actually is to a computer, the different tasks vision models perform, the convolution operation that makes CNNs work, and why almost nobody trains one from scratch anymore.

  • Why an image is just a grid (or three) of numbers, nothing more
  • The difference between classification, detection, and segmentation
  • How a convolution kernel actually computes a feature map, step by step
  • Why fine-tuning a pretrained model needs far less data than training from scratch

Quick reference: core tasks

TaskOutputAnswers
