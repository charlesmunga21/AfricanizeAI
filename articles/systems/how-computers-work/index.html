How Computers Work

Every app you use — including a database — is just a program asking a computer for two things over and over: "do some work" and "remember something." Underneath that simple request sit four physical parts working together: a CPU that does the work, a tiny cache next to it for things it needs right now, RAM for things it needs soon, and a disk for things it needs to keep even after the power goes off.

This module walks through those four parts in plain language — no electronics degree required — and then ties them together with something you'll actually build with: how real databases like SQLite and Postgres use this exact same hardware to answer your queries fast. Ideas here are drawn from Brendan Gregg's book Systems Performance and PlanetScale's engineering blog. Every submodule has a small interactive model to play with, and a short quiz at the end.

  • What a computer actually is, in terms of the four parts that do all the work
  • What the CPU does, and why it can only do one tiny step at a time — just extremely fast
  • Why CPUs have a "cache," and why that one idea explains almost everything about computer speed
  • Why RAM is fast but forgets everything, and how a computer decides what to keep in it
  • Why disks are slow but permanent, and what that trade-off actually costs in real time
  • How SQLite and Postgres — two very different databases — both lean on RAM and disk to be fast and safe
