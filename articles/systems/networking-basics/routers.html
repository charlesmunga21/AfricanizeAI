Routers: The Path Data Takes

A packet almost never travels straight from your device to its destination in one jump. It passes through a chain of devices called routers, each one looking at the packet's address and forwarding it one step closer to where it's going.

Like a postal sorting system

Your letter doesn't go directly from your mailbox to the recipient's — it passes through a local sorting office, maybe a regional hub, then a hub closer to the destination, then finally local delivery. Routers do the same job for data: your home router hands the packet to your internet provider's router, which hands it toward the destination's network, which hands it to the right server — often five, ten, or more hops in total.

Each router only needs to know "which way is closer"

No single router needs to know the entire path in advance. It just needs to know, for a given address, which direction gets a packet closer to home — the same way a sorting clerk doesn't need to know your exact street, just which regional hub to send it toward next. Each hop narrows things down a little more, until the packet lands at the right doorstep.
