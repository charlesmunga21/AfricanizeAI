Networking Basics

Every time you open a website, send a message, or stream a video, your device is having a conversation with another computer somewhere else — sometimes on the other side of the planet — and it usually takes less than a second. This module explains, in the plainest language possible, how that's even possible. No prior knowledge of networks assumed.

We'll build it up one small idea at a time — what a network even is, how your data actually travels, how a name like "google.com" becomes a real address, and why some connections feel instant while others feel slow. Every submodule has a small interactive model and a short quiz.

  • What a network is, and why "the internet" is really a network of networks
  • How your data gets broken into small pieces called packets before it travels
  • How a website name gets turned into a real address (DNS)
  • How a packet actually finds its way, hopping between machines called routers
  • Why some things feel instant and others feel slow — latency vs. bandwidth
  • Watching it all happen at once: what really happens when you load a web page
