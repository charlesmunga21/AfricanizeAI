DNS: The Internet's Phonebook

You never type a server's actual IP address into your browser. You type a name — wikipedia.org, google.com. Something has to translate that friendly name into the real address before your device can connect to anything. That something is DNS, the Domain Name System.

Like looking someone up before you call them

Think about calling a friend: you don't dial their raw phone number from memory, you look up their name in your contacts app, which hands you the actual number to dial. DNS does exactly that for the internet — you give it a name, and it hands back the address, before your device connects to anything.

Your device remembers, so it doesn't have to ask twice

Just like the caches you met in the CPU Cache and Databases submodules, DNS answers get cached. Once your device has looked up a name, it remembers the answer for a while, so visiting the same site again doesn't mean asking DNS all over — it's instant, straight from memory, exactly the same "check nearby first" idea you've already seen twice in this course.
