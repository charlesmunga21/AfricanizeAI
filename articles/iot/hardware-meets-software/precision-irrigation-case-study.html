Precision Irrigation Pivots

A center-pivot irrigator — the machine that creates those circular green fields you see from an airplane — is a genuinely good case study, because commercial systems have already built the entire pipeline from the last submodule, at scale, for years.

What's actually on the machine

Lindsay Corporation's FieldNET Pivot Watch is a real product built around exactly this idea: a solar-powered telemetry unit that mounts onto a center pivot — any pivot, regardless of age or brand — and radios back the machine's own status: whether water is flowing, its current position in the field, and its direction and speed. Valmont's competing Valley brand offers a similar system, AgSense 365, which extends beyond monitoring into remote control of pivots, pumps, and moisture probes from one app.

On the research side, a 2025 study published in a peer-reviewed sensors journal took this a step further: mounting soil moisture sensors ("smart stakes") directly in the field, communicating over Bluetooth Low Energy to a rotating receiver mounted on the pivot boom itself as it sweeps overhead — collecting moisture readings across the whole field, not just at one fixed point.

Why remote monitoring matters here specifically

A pivot can be hundreds of meters long, watering a field far from the farmhouse, and a stopped or misaligned pivot wastes water, wastes electricity, and can under- or over-water a section of crop for hours before anyone notices by driving out to look. A telemetry unit turns "drive out and check" into a push notification. That's the entire value proposition of an otherwise fairly ordinary embedded system: a few sensors, a cheap radio, and a scheduled read-and-send loop.
